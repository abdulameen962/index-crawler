Frequently Asked Questions & Methodology

Why should I execute replication orders via Limit Orders?

The Nigerian Exchange (NGX) exhibits intraday liquidity variations. Executing with Limit Orders placed after the trading day or at the morning open (10:00 AM WAT) guarantees that your trades fill at official End-of-Day settlement prices used in this replication model, completely avoiding intraday market order spread slippage.

How does the Greedy Cash Sweep eliminate unallocated cash?

Standard index calculations round fractional shares down, which often leaves significant residual cash (e.g., ₦19,000 unspent). Our algorithm applies a Greedy Tracking-Error Cash Sweep: it takes whatever residual cash remains and iteratively allocates additional shares to the most underweight equities until residual cash is strictly less than the cheapest stock price in the index.

Why is there a 15% equity concentration cap?

Without a cap, ultra-large market cap equities (like SEPLAT or Dangote Cement) would dominate 50% to 70% of the entire fund. Capping any single equity at 15% ensures balanced diversification across all index constituents while proportionally redistributing excess weight.

What transaction fees are factored into the calculation?

The default 3.0% transaction fee models all statutory and intermediary charges on the NGX, including Stockbrokerage Commission (typically 1.35%), SEC Regulatory Fee (0.30%), CSCS Transaction Fee (0.30%), Stamp Duty (0.075%), NGX Trade Fee (0.12%), and VAT.
